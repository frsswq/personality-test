Design study

Twelve marks
for personality-test

The shipped mark is a nested P. It says personal perspective to the people who drew it, and it reads as a generic letterform to everyone else. These twelve alternatives replace the word shape with an idea. Each one is built on a 40 by 40 construction grid from whole shapes, circles, radii, and one stroke weight, in the tradition of Yasaburo Kuwayama and Jens Muller: heavy silhouettes, simple geometry, and negative space that carries meaning.

Guides show the construction, the axes, and the inscribed circle, not the artwork.

Rules these marks follow

Construction first

Every curve is a true circle or a true quarter arc. No freehand path, and no line that only looks straight.

One weight

Stroked marks use 4 units on the 40 unit grid, with round caps and joins. Filled marks carry no stroke.

Two values

Each mark must hold at 28 pixels, at 16 pixels, and reversed out of a solid field. The strip and the cards test all three.

Meaning from the product

The ideas come from the test itself: a set of scores, four dimensions, depth, reflection, and a result that is not a type.

Negative space earns its place

Where a mark uses a counter, such as the crescent void or the removed lens, the void carries the second half of the idea.

Light mode only

The mark is one ink on a near-white page. The reversed chip checks the solid field, and no dark palette exists.
